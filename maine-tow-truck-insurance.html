Tow Truck Insurance in Maine

Maine splits by weight: $750,000 CSL for tow trucks that are commercial motor vehicles at 26,001+ lbs, and $350,000 CSL for for-hire towing below that threshold. The state sets no on-hook, garagekeepers, or cargo minimum.

Verified from state law
Auto liability
Weight-split under 29-A MRSA § 1611(2): $750,000 CSL for tow trucks that are 'commercial motor vehicles' in intrastate commerce — i.e., GVWR/GVW or GCWR of 26,001+ lbs, buses, or hazmat-placarded vehicles (§ 1611(2)(G) + § 101(17) definition). $350,000 CSL for for-hire towing below that threshold, as for-hire freight transport in intrastate-exempt service (§ 1611(2)(A)).
On-hook (in-tow)
No statewide minimum — your city/county or rotation agreement may still set one
Filings
Insurance policy, indemnity bond, self-insurance declaratory judgment, or ICC-certified-carrier policy/bond presented to the Secretary of State (BMV) before registration of a hire vehicle (§ 1611(1)); insurer must give at least 30 days' notice of cancellation to the Secretary of State; registration certificate and plates suspended/revoked if coverage lapses (§ 1611(3)). No Form E.

Weight / class variation: § 1611(2)(G): intrastate commercial motor vehicles (26,001+ lbs GVWR/GVW or GCWR, buses, hazmat-placarded). § 1611(2)(A): for-hire freight in intrastate-exempt service. Maine's § 101(17) CMV threshold is 26,001+ lbs, not the federal 10,001-lb threshold — interstate wreckers at 10,001+ lbs pick up the federal $750,000 through the tool's federal layer.

Both § 1611 paragraphs govern tow trucks; neither is the single answer. (G) is unambiguous for 26,001+ lbs. For lighter wreckers, (G) cannot apply (they are not § 101(17) CMVs), and (A) is the only § 1611 paragraph that can cover them: § 1611(1) bars registering any hire vehicle without insurance, and (A) covers 'for-hire transportation vehicles for transporting freight or merchandise but not passengers in intrastate-exempt service.' The exempt-service hook: emergency towing of an accidentally wrecked/disabled motor vehicle is expressly exempt from federal jurisdiction under 49 U.S.C. § 13506(b)(3). Caveats: the statute never names tow trucks; the (A) application is a statutory reading, not a BMV/court-confirmed position. Non-emergency towing (planned transport, repossession, impound of an operable vehicle) may fall outside the § 13506(b)(3) emergency-towing exemption, so (A)'s exempt-service qualifier is less certain for those operations. (G) was added in 2025 (PL 2025, c. 107), in force as of 2026-10-14 (applies to policies on/after Jan. 1, 2026). No state on-hook, garagekeepers, or cargo minimum (§ 1611 covers liability only). Do-not-confuse: § 1861's perishable-cargo provision concerns the towed vehicle's own cargo insurance, not a tow-operator minimum; § 1605-B's $500 towing/storage coverage is an obligation on the towed vehicle's own policy. § 1611(1)(D) still references the obsolete 49 CFR 1043.2 / ICC certification — an anachronism, not a live Form E requirement.
