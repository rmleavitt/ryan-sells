Tow Truck Insurance in South Carolina

South Carolina licenses tow operators as DMV Certificate-of-Compliance carriers: $750,000 per incident at 10,000+ lbs GVWR, $300,000 under, plus $5,000 cargo per vehicle. The state sets no tow-specific on-hook or garagekeepers minimum.

South Carolina state minimums

Verified from state law
Auto liability
Intrastate for-hire (DMV Certificate of Compliance, Exhibit A): $750,000 per incident at 10,000+ lbs GVWR; $300,000 per incident under 10,000 lbs GVWR, non-hazardous. Hazmat: $5,000,000 / $1,000,000 per incident.
On-hook (in-tow)
No statewide minimum — your city/county or rotation agreement may still set one
Cargo
$5,000 per vehicle (DMV CoC, Class E-LC, Exhibit A).
Filings
Form E (liability) and Form H (cargo, if E-LC) filed with SCDMV by the insurer — not ORS; ACORD certificate not accepted.

Weight / class variation: SC splits at 10,000 lbs GVWR (≥10,000 vs <10,000), unlike the federal 10,001 split — a wrecker at exactly 10,000 lbs GVWR falls in SC's $750,000 tier.

Tow operators are DMV Certificate-of-Compliance carriers, NOT PSC-certificated carriers: S.C. Code § 56-3-661 requires every for-hire motor-vehicle carrier of property except household-goods and hazmat-waste carriers to hold a Class E Certificate of Compliance from the DMV, and the DMV CoC Exhibit A schedule governs. The PSC regs. 103-172/103-173 govern PSC-certificated carriers only — 103-172's $500,000 schedule for under-10,000-lb carriers does not apply to tow operators, and 103-173's cargo schedule does not either. No statewide tow-specific on-hook or garagekeepers minimum (SCHP rotation-only figures under 38-600 are separate). The $50,000 garagekeepers claim circulated for SC does not appear in SC law. Independent-contractor test: Common-law right-to-control test, 4 factors weighed with equal force — Wilkinson v. Palmetto State Transp. Co., 382 S.C. 295, 676 S.E.2d 700 (2009) (overruling Dawkins' pro-employment weighting). The WC statute itself (§ 42-1-130) defines "employee" broadly (contract of hire, express/implied) without listing IC factors; the test comes from WC case law. Unemployment insurance uses the same test: under S.C. Code § 41-27-230(1)(b), "employment" turns on the usual common-law rules, and the SC Supreme Court in Kilgore Group v. S.C. Employment Security Commission applies the same four right-to-control factors as workers' comp — direct evidence of control, furnishing of equipment, method of payment, and right to fire — with no ABC test in the UI statute. (Equal-force weighting in UI not confirmed — Kilgore lists the same four factors without stating equal-force weighting.)
